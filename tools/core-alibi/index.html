Core & IMS health

Faults are things the core or IMS did wrong. Warnings cost packets but did not end service on their own.

How the verdict is decided

  1. Core or IMS is blamed only for something it did: an NF crash or restart, SMF–UPF association loss, the UPF discarding packets (tun interface down), the network releasing a PDU session, a reject, or IMS ending a call that no phone hung up.
  2. Phone detached: the phone sent a NAS Deregistration Request (power-off, airplane mode, low battery).
  3. Radio side: the gNB released the UE, failed to set up its bearers, reset, or lost its N2 link, or the phone simply went silent while the gNB kept it "connected" and never told the core.
  4. Calls are judged by who sent the BYE and whether media was still arriving from each phone at that moment (from RTPEngine's per-stream last-packet times).
  5. Not visible here: gNB internals (RLF, scheduling, HARQ) and throughput. The core logs only show what reached the core. Ping logs tell when data actually stopped.
